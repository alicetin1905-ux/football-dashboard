Methodology. Each team gets an attack and defense strength (their recent goals scored/conceded, split by home/away venue, relative to their league's own average), which combine into an expected goal count for each side of a fixture. From those two expected counts, a full score-probability matrix (0–0, 1–0, 0–1, ...) gives properly joint win/BTTS/combined probabilities — unlike just multiplying two unrelated historical rates, this correctly links the two: a big mismatch predicts both a likely win and a correspondingly lower BTTS chance, because the weaker side's expected goals are genuinely low. The two teams' goal counts are still treated as statistically independent of each other, a known, accepted simplification of this model (not a true joint distribution). The Rank by control switches what's ranked, all from the same underlying score matrix: away win + BTTS, home win + BTTS, BTTS + Over 2.5 (both teams score and the match produces three or more goals total — not tied to either team winning), Home/Draw + BTTS (a "double chance" bet — the home team just needs to avoid defeat, not win outright — and both teams score), a plain Home win / Away win / Draw with no BTTS attached — a single, easier condition that clears more often than any of the combo modes, at the cost of not carrying the BTTS angle at all (those three show — instead of a BTTS figure, since there isn't one) — or Best of all modes, which doesn't apply one fixed bet to every match: for each fixture it picks whichever of the other seven has the single highest probability, tags the row with which one that was, and ranks the whole list by that. Since every mode reads off the same score matrix, comparing their raw probabilities against each other this way is coherent — it answers "what's the single most likely outcome for this match, across everything tracked here", not a comparison of unrelated things. Picking a maximum across several estimates this way has a well-known bias, though — a "winner's curse" that favors whichever estimate got the luckiest noise, not necessarily the genuinely best bet — so its combined probability is shrunk by a factor measured from the Results backtest each refresh (actual hit rate ÷ average predicted probability among its own past picks), rather than shown raw. Fixtures where either team has fewer than 5 recent matches on record are flagged "low sample", since a small sample makes for a noisy goal-scoring average. Alerts are always based on away win + BTTS, regardless of the selected ranking. The Home/Away form badges on each row are the matches that average comes from — the home team's own home matches, oldest to newest, and the away team's own away matches. The Results view backtests the last week of already-played matches: each prediction there is computed from only the matches that came before that kickoff, so it's the call the model would actually have made going in, checked against what really happened.
